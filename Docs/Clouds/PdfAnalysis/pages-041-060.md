## Page 44

**Section header:** Nubis³ / A Brief (Nubis-Centric) History of Cloud Rendering

**Diagram slide (black background):** Cone Step Mapping explained. Camera icon at right,
horizontal view ray pointing left over a grey cloud-topped heightfield silhouette. Three
open red circles on the ray each drop a dashed vertical arrow down to an open white circle
on the cloud surface below (the conservatively-known nearest surface distance), and dashed
diagonal lines (the cones) extend up-left from those surface points past the ray. Where the
ray finally meets the cloud, four solid red dots are tightly clustered (fine steps at the
intersection). Illustrates iteratively stepping by the cone-safe distance until close to
the surface, then refining.

**Citation (exact):** Jonathan "Lone Sock" Dummer, Cone Step Mapping: An Iterative Ray
Heightfield Intersection Algorithm. 2006.

## Page 48

**Section header:** Nubis³ / A Brief (Nubis-Centric) History of Cloud Rendering

**Text slide (black background), titled at left:** "Nubis Volumetric Ray-March Procedure"

**Pseudo-code (exact, indentation as shown; the line "Start a march along a ray:" is boxed
for emphasis):**

```
For each Pixel:
  ↳ Start a march along a ray:
      ↳ For each step along the ray:
            Sample Density
            Sample Light Energy
            Integrate into Pixel data
            Determine step size and take the next step
```

This is the canonical Nubis march loop: per-pixel ray, per-step density sample, light
energy sample, integration, then adaptive step-size decision.

## Page 50

**Section header:** Nubis³ / A Brief (Nubis-Centric) History of Cloud Rendering

**Diagram slide (black background):** Resolution-vs-distance scheme for the HFW cloud
render targets. A camera icon sits at the right; a dashed horizontal ray extends left. A
bracket spanning the first stretch of the ray from the camera is labeled (exact) **"200 m"**.
Two checkerboard-patterned buffer rectangles hang below markers on the ray:

- Far rectangle (beyond 200 m): labeled **960 px** on the vertical axis and **540 px** on
  the horizontal axis (fine checkerboard — the higher-resolution buffer used for distant
  clouds).
- Near rectangle (within the 200 m bracket): labeled **480 px** vertical, **270 px**
  horizontal (coarse checkerboard — quarter-res buffer for near/flythrough clouds).

I.e. clouds within ~200 m of the camera are marched into a 480x270-class buffer, distant
clouds into a 960x540-class buffer (both then upscaled; cf. the "Clouds - Upscaled" buffers
on page 54, and the RG_FLOAT_16 [960x540] buffer format there).

**Citation (exact):** Andrew Schneider. "Nubis, Evolved: Real-time Volumetric Clouds for
Skies, Environments, and VFX". *ACM SIGGRAPH*. Vancouver, BC: ACM SIGGRAPH, 2022. Web. 2022.

## Page 51

**Section header:** Nubis³ / A Brief (Nubis-Centric) History of Cloud Rendering

**Table slide (exact contents)** — capability comparison of the two pre-Nubis3 modeling
approaches, "Vertical Profile Method" (the classic Nubis 2D-map + vertical-profile clouds)
vs "Envelope Method" (the HFW flythrough envelope clouds):

| | Vertical Profile Method | Envelope Method |
|---|---|---|
| Evolution | Yes (green) | Pseudomotion Only (orange) |
| Time Of Day | Yes (green) | Yes (green) |
| Lightning | Yes (green) | No (red) |
| High Frame-rates | Yes (green) | Yes (green) |
| Flight-Capable | No (red) | Yes (green) |
| Freeform Modeling | No (red) | No (red) |

**Point:** neither legacy method supports freeform modeling, and each lacks something the
other has (evolution/lightning vs flight) — motivating the new voxel-based Nubis3 approach.

## Range summary

Pages 41–51 close the history section: HFW (Nubis2, "Oro" clouds) used a per-pixel
ray-march (page 48 loop) accelerated by Cone Step Mapping + Distance Step Mapping
(pages 43–45), cutting the high-res cloud pass from 4.2 ms to 1.3 ms (pages 46–47),
rendered into checkerboard-updated low-res buffers with a distance split at 200 m
(480x270-class near buffer, 960x540-class far buffer, pages 49–50); but neither the
Vertical Profile Method nor the Envelope Method offered freeform modeling (page 51).
Pages 52–55 introduce the multi-voxel cloud renderer prototype (march confined to voxel
AABBs; Attr+Color buffer pipeline with prepass/low-res/blur/upscale/dome stages,
RG_FLOAT_16 960x540). Pages 56–60 show the Burning Shores trailer workflow: previz proxy
meshes art-direct the cloudscape, realized as real-time volumetrics on PS5, with the plan
being to support ALL capabilities of both legacy methods plus freeform modeling.
