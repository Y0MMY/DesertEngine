## Page 1
**Content:** Full-bleed reference render (caption bottom-left: "Decima Engine, 2023"). A single sculpted cumulus cloud shaped like the Guerrilla Games logo (two interlocking chevrons), rendered in-engine at golden hour.
**Visual character:**
- Shape: dense, billowy cumulus with clear medium-scale cauliflower lobes and fine crisp curl/wisp detail along all silhouettes; the shape holds a deliberate authored macro form (proof of art-directable voxel modeling).
- Edges: sharp, high-frequency detail; small wispy tendrils breaking off the silhouette; no mushy alpha falloff.
- Lighting: low warm sun from screen-left/behind. Lit faces are warm peach/cream (light salmon-white); self-shadowed undersides and crevices are desaturated mauve/grey-lavender — notably NOT dark grey: shadow regions are clearly tinted by the sky (cool violet-pink ambient).
- Sky: gradient from slate blue-grey at top (with thin stratus streaks tinted pink-orange on their undersides) through dusty lavender to a pale warm haze near the bottom of frame. Thin background cirrus/stratus catch orange rim light.
- Overall color story: warm direct light + cool-violet ambient fill; the ambient term visibly dominates the shadowed cloud regions and keeps them luminous rather than black.
**Formulas/constants:** none.

## Page 3
**Content:** Section "A Brief (Nubis-Centric) History of Cloud Rendering". Slide shows a reproduction of the SIGGRAPH 2011 talk/paper page "Clouds in the skies of Rio" — Andrew P. Schneider, Trevor G. Thomson, Matthew S. Wilson, Blue Sky Studios. Caption bottom-left: "SIGGRAPH, 2011".
**Readable content of the embedded paper (small type; partially legible):** sections Introduction / Synthesizing Clouds / Workflow / Volumetric Rendering / Conclusions. Legible points: three cloud types were built (cumulus "heavy billowing", stratocumulus, stratus "wispy"); clouds were modeled from spheres converted into voxel grids of density values and processed with noise deformers for billowy and wispy patterns; rendering used "SmogVox", a proprietary ray-tracing renderer (part of CGI Studio), modified with an efficient multiple-scattering approximation, optimized ray-march step sizes based on density/density gradient, optimized shadow-ray points, and higher-frequency noise re-added at render time controlled by position/density/height-gradient/velocity fields. Conclusions mention "over 534 shots" and memory on the order of hundreds of MB for low-res clouds up to ~4 GB for high-resolution clouds. (Fine print at this resolution — treat exact figures as approximate except where quoted.)
**Relevance:** establishes the lineage: voxel modeling + density-gradient-aware ray marching + re-added render-time detail noise predate Nubis and return in Nubis³.
**Formulas/constants:** none legible beyond the above.

## Page 13
**Content:** Pseudo-code slide — "Nubis Volumetric Ray-March Procedure" (same 2022 citation at bottom).
**Pseudo-code (exact):**
```
For each Pixel:
  Start a march along a ray:
    For each step along the ray:
      Sample Density        <- (boxed/highlighted on this slide)
      Sample Light Energy
      Integrate into Pixel data
      Determine step size and take the next step
```
"Sample Density" is highlighted with a box — the talk is about to zoom into the density/modeling half of the loop.
**Formulas/constants:** none beyond the procedure.

## Page 19
**Content:** "Vertical Profile Method NDF's" (same 2022 citation).
**Exact NDF stack labels (5 fields):** "Min Height", "Max Height", "Coverage", "Bottom Type", "Top Type".
**Right diagram:** two horizontal lines labeled "Cloud Max Height" and "Cloud Min Height" with a double arrow between them. Below: two greyscale gradient lookup textures multiplied together (an "×" between them):
- "Cloud Top Type" texture: vertical axis "Height", horizontal axis = top type; contains a white region eroding to black with height — different columns give different top profiles (billowy step-like white shape).
- "Cloud Bottom Type" texture: vertical axis "Height", horizontal = bottom type; a smooth gradient (dark at bottom-left rising to light) shaping the base.
**Formula (exact):**
```c
float dimensional_profile = vertical_profile * cloud_coverage;
```
where `vertical_profile` = CloudTopType(height, top_type) × CloudBottomType(height, bottom_type) sampled between the per-column Min/Max heights, and `cloud_coverage` comes from the Coverage NDF.
**Formulas/constants:** the line above; 5 named NDFs.

## Page 20
**Content:** "Envelope Method NDF's" (same 2022 citation).
**Exact NDF stack labels (4 fields):** "Min Height", "Max Height", "Type", "Density".
**Right diagram:** a dome/arc labeled "Cloud Max Height" over a horizontal line labeled "Cloud Min Height", with a grey lumpy cloud silhouette filling the space between — the envelope is a dome-bounded region between min height plane and max height arc; the cloud fills it and is faded toward the boundary.
**Formula (exact):**
```c
float dimensional_profile = bottom_gradient * top_gradient * edge_gradient;
```
i.e., for envelope clouds the base profile is the product of three fade gradients: from the bottom plane, from the top (dome) bound, and from the lateral edge of the envelope.
**Formulas/constants:** the line above; 4 named NDFs.
